# RFC Candidates

Candidate RFCs surfaced by owner-requested capture and `frame-situation`
escalations. Each entry represents a pattern, gap, or design question that
recurred enough to warrant community input before implementation.

**How entries arrive here:** when an owner explicitly decides to record an
out-of-scope finding that looks RFC-shaped, or through a `frame-situation`
escalation. This is the register for that RFC candidate.

**How entries leave:** when a candidate is accepted into `docs/rfc/`, update
`Disposition` to `→ RFC-NNNN`. When a candidate is rejected or absorbed,
note the reason.

| Every authoring skill in `experience-design` validates its own output against its own contract; none reads another skill's output for contradiction. Measured across one engagement: of 52 findings from three independent cold reviews, roughly 25 were cross-artifact contradictions or declared counts that disagreed with the corpus — including a packet-wide claim of "eleven gate identifiers, both surfaces" when the documentation surface publishes **94 across 14 files** (one of which defines them as reader-facing vocabulary), and a hand-authored canvas composed on a light ground that the same packet's own information architecture had placed in the **dark** hero band, voiding all fourteen of its contrast measurements. Both were locally correct in the artifact that made them and false against the set. Candidate: a packet-scoped consistency check over `docs/design/**` that fails when a declared count contradicts the corpus, when a named invariant or state set differs between artifacts, or when a superseded claim survives elsewhere. Scope it to numerals and named invariants, never to prose — a check that tries to judge whether two paragraphs agree will produce false positives and be disabled.
