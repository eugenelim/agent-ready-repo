## Verdict
SHIP WITH CHANGES

## Summary
Four adopter-facing text surfaces introduce a `visual_target` frontmatter key: a template section, its derived guide excerpt, the `converge` instruction that writes it, and a published changelog Highlight. The template comment is the strongest surface — it names the canonical source, the absent-field reading, and a privacy limit. The dominant weakness is that the field arrives with no stated purpose and no instruction for the *not-yet-confirmed* case, while the guide prose never mentions it at all and the changelog bullet spends two of its three sentences on schema detail and a disclaimer. (Lenses: these are prose/documentation surfaces, so platform-fit, handle-all-states, accessibility and reduced-motion have no applicable target; grounded fit, cross-surface coherence and the marketing-clarity lens on the changelog bullet are the live ones.)

## Findings

### 🟥 Blockers

**1. An adopter who has not confirmed a target is told to invent a confirmation date.** Where: `packs/experience-design/.apm/skills/creative-direction/assets/creative-direction-template.md:100` (`**Confirmation record:** <YYYY-MM-DD> — <where the confirmation was recorded>`), against the global fill instruction at `:23–24` ("Fill the angle-bracket prompts and delete this comment") and the guide's caption at `guides/experience-design/how-to/establish-design-intent.md:239` ("The agent replaces every `<…>`"). What's wrong: coherence — the section comment says to fill this line "only once a human has confirmed the target" (`:88–90`), but two other instructions say every angle-bracket prompt gets replaced, and neither says what the line should read when the disposition is `none` or `unconfirmed`, so the most likely outcome is a fabricated date that makes the artifact assert a human confirmation that never happened. Fix: in the template comment only (the placeholder string is pinned; do not touch it), state the not-yet-confirmed disposition explicitly — e.g. "when `visual_target` is `none` or `unconfirmed`, leave this line as the shipped placeholder" — and add the matching exception to the global "replaces every `<…>`" expectation. Re-derive the guide excerpt from the template; do not edit `establish-design-intent.md` directly.

### 🟧 Majors

**2. The guide's prose never mentions the field, so nobody is told what to do with it.** Where: `guides/experience-design/how-to/establish-design-intent.md:95–132` — `visual_target` appears only inside the fenced excerpt (`:149–153`, `:236`) and in no sentence of surrounding prose; the human gate at `:121` ("**You decide:** Approve a specific aesthetic direction…"), the `Check (grounded)` at `:124` and `Watch out for` at `:127` are all silent on it. What's wrong: coherence — the one moment a reader could move a target from `unconfirmed` to `confirmed` is the `approve-aesthetic-direction` gate, and the guide does not connect the two, so the key reads as unexplained metadata. Fix: add one authored sentence near `:121` saying that confirming a visual target is part of that gate, that confirming it is what makes the disposition `confirmed`, and where the confirmation gets recorded; optionally extend `Check (grounded)` to ask whether the recorded disposition matches what a human actually confirmed.

**3. Nowhere on an adopter-facing surface does it say the field is a record only.** Where: template `:13–17` and `:82–90`; guide excerpt `:149–153`; `references/converge.md:56–61`. What's wrong: grounded fit — the change's defining property (nothing reads the field, nothing is gated on it) appears only in the changelog, so a first-time adopter cannot tell whether a wrong or missing value breaks a downstream step, and will treat a free record as a compliance obligation. Fix: add one clause to the template's `visual_target` comment stating that the key is a record for human readers and no step reads or gates on it, then re-derive the excerpt.

**4. "Approved" and "confirmed" are used as two different vocabularies for the same question, with no stated relationship.** Where: template section heading `## Approved visual target` (`:78`) versus the `unconfirmed | confirmed` set (`:17`); `references/converge.md:84` ("When an approved visual target exists, write the selected direction's compositional commitments into the doc here") versus `:56–61`. What's wrong: coherence — it is unresolved whether an `unconfirmed` target counts as "approved", and therefore whether `converge` writes compositional commitments off a target no human has confirmed. Fix: in `converge.md:84`, state the condition in the new vocabulary — name which dispositions license compositional commitments — and align the template's `## Compositional commitments` comment (`:231–233`, same phrase "when an approved visual target exists") with it.

**5. The privacy limit lives only in a comment the skill is told to delete.** Where: template `:89–90` ("never a person's name, handle, or contact detail") against `:24` ("delete this comment"), with no counterpart in `references/converge.md:56–61`. What's wrong: coherence — `converge` is the writer, and its instruction says to name the target, what binds and what adapts, but never carries the no-personal-identifier rule, so the constraint disappears exactly where it is enforced. Fix: restate the no-personal-identifier limit in `converge.md`'s disposition paragraph, and have it also instruct filling `**Confirmation record:**` with date and location when it writes `confirmed`.

**6. Changelog bullet: two of three sentences are contributor-facing schema detail.** Where: `docs/product/changelog.md:147`, specifically "A `visual_target` key on the direction artifact holds one of `none`, `unconfirmed`, or `confirmed`, and `converge` writes it every time it runs". What's wrong: marketing clarity — *painkiller-first* / *deletion audit*; the entry's own header requires Highlights bullets to be outcome-led and "Rewrite for users, not contributors" with "everything else in the entry stays technical" (`:33`, `:40–41`, `:57–58`), and the field name, the literal value set and the writing operation are the technical half. Fix: keep sentence one, cut the value enumeration from the Highlight, and move the key name, closed set and `converge` behaviour into an `### Added` section of the same entry.

**7. Changelog bullet: the closing sentence tells the reader the change does not change anything they can do.** Where: `docs/product/changelog.md:147`, "Nothing reads the field and nothing is gated on it: it is a record you can act on when you want to, and ignore when you do not." What's wrong: marketing clarity — *five-second scan* and *deletion audit*; the header admits a Highlight only when "the release changes what a consumer can do" (`:38–39`), and this sentence answers the reader's "should I care?" with "not necessarily", which is the weakest sentence in the bullet. Fix: delete it from the Highlight and replace the lost value with the action the record enables — e.g. that at the approval gate you can now see whether the target was actually signed off, instead of inferring it; keep the "nothing is gated on it" fact in the technical `### Added` section where it belongs.

### 🟨 Minors

**8. The section opens with "Optional." and later says do not leave it off.** Where: template `:80` ("Optional. If no target exists, write \"none\" and continue") versus `:84–85` ("record it deliberately rather than leaving it off"). What's wrong: clarity — the first word of the section reads as making the new key optional, which is the opposite of the instruction four lines down. Fix: scope the word — say the *target* is optional while the disposition is always recorded — then re-derive the excerpt.

**9. The absent-field reading is stated but never justified, and is wrong for the common case.** Where: template `:84–85`, "An absent `visual_target` reads as `unconfirmed`". What's wrong: clarity — for a hand-authored direction with no target at all, the absent-field reading says "a target is recorded but nobody confirmed it", which is false; the conservative-default reasoning (never silently claim a confirmation) is the thing that makes the rule make sense and it is missing. Fix: add a half-sentence giving the reason — the reading is deliberately the cautious one, so a missing key can never be read as a confirmation.

**10. Two adjacent keys both answer "did a human confirm?" with no stated difference.** Where: template `:8–17` (`status: proposed | selected` immediately above `visual_target`). What's wrong: clarity — both comments turn on human confirmation, and nothing says they are confirmations of different things or that `selected` with `unconfirmed` is a legitimate combination. Fix: one clause noting `status` records confirmation of the direction and `visual_target` confirmation of the target, and that they move independently.

**11. The release entry has a Highlights block and no conventional change section.** Where: `docs/product/changelog.md:143–147`, compared with `:149–157` where 4.1.1 carries `### Highlights` plus `### Fixed`. What's wrong: coherence with the file's own format — the header expects the technical record to live alongside the Highlight, and there is none. Fix: add `### Added` with the schema facts pulled out of findings 6 and 7.

### ⚪ Nits

**12. "over `none`, `unconfirmed` and `confirmed`" is terse where plain words were asked for.** Where: template `:83–84`. Fix: "one of" reads plainer than "over", and saying no other value is valid states the closed set outright.

## What's working

- The template comment already does the three hardest things an adopter needs: it names the frontmatter key as canonical over the body lines, states the absent-field reading explicitly, and carries a privacy limit on the confirmation record. That structure is worth preserving through the fixes above.
- Separating "provenance for a human reader" from anything binding — the `bind nothing on their own` clause at template `:86–87` — stops the four body lines from being mistaken for constraints, and it sits in the right place, next to the lines it governs.
- The changelog bullet's first sentence is genuinely outcome-led and passes the specificity check: "tell that apart from never having asked" names a real distinction an adopter recognises, and the bullet contains none of the AI-smell vocabulary. Keep that sentence close to as-is.
- `converge.md:56–61` maps each of the three values to a concrete condition rather than restating the set, so the writer has an unambiguous selection rule.
