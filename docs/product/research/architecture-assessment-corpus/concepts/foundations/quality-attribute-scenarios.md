# Quality-attribute scenarios — source packet

## Material claims

| Claim | Synthesis | Sources |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QA-1 | Broad quality labels need operational scenarios before architecture adequacy can be evaluated. | S1, S2, S3 |
| QA-2 | A scenario should connect stimulus, environment, response mechanism, and measurable outcome. | S1, S2, S4 |
| QA-3 | Quality mechanisms create trade-offs and require stakeholder priority. | S1, S3, S4 |

## Sources

- S1: [SEI ATAM method](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/atam-method-for-architecture-evaluation/) — quality scenarios and architectural approaches.
- S2: [SEI ATAM collection](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/architecture-tradeoff-analysis-method-collection/) — scenario elicitation and analysis.
- S3: [ISO/IEC 25010:2023](https://www.iso.org/standard/78176.html) — software product quality model.
- S4: [Azure Well-Architected Framework](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/well-architected/what-is-well-architected-framework) — workload quality trade-offs.

## Counter-evidence and downgrade factors

Exact scenario fields vary by method. Numeric targets can imply false precision
when demand, risk tolerance, or user impact is not known.

## Known unknowns

The cheapest representative exercise differs by workload and environment access.
