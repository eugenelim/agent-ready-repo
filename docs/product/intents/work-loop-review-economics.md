# Work-loop review economics

- **Status:** Draft
- **Kind:** outcome
- **Level:** feature
- **Scale:** app
- **Maturity:** brownfield
- **Parent intent:** [Work-loop delivery efficiency](work-loop-delivery-efficiency.md)

## Outcome

- **Steerable input:** Reduce review-stage wall-clock time and model tokens by replacing repeated fresh broad review with consequence-bound blocking, focused re-review of changed findings, and revision-bound reuse of still-valid review results.
- **Lagging outcome:** Representative repository changes reach a defensible stopping point with fewer review, adjudication, and repair cycles because review effort stays proportional to the material risk introduced by the change.
- **Guardrail:** Escaped material defects and post-review rework do not worsen. Security, privacy, data-loss, migration, mixed-version, public-contract, destructive-operation, and human-approval violations remain blocking, and every accepted residual risk is explicit and owner-authorized.

## Opportunity

The current review contract can keep sampling new findings after the relevant contract and implementation bytes are stable. Each new finding can trigger adjudication, repair, gates, and another broad review, so the cost of seeking Clean is not limited to reviewer time. The parent intent records an 11-round example with 87 findings, 72 sustained fixes, and 9 further defects found while checking fixes, plus an earlier approximately two-hour, approximately $60 session dominated by reviewer fan-out. A stopping contract that preserves material assurance while avoiding broad resampling would reduce both direct review cost and the work generated by review itself.

## Boundary

- Includes reviewer scope, blocking-severity evidence, finding adjudication, repair-triggered re-review, revision or byte-digest validity, stopping semantics, and review-stage wall-clock and token measurement.
- The candidate policy may use one broad baseline review, then limit re-review to sustained findings, their fixes, affected contract bytes, and demonstrated dependency drift. A blocker must identify a plausible material consequence against an accepted requirement, protected risk class, or repository invariant.
- A revision-bound Clean result may be reused only while its declared subject bytes and governing contract remain unchanged. Relevant changes invalidate the result and reopen the matching review scope.
- Owners may explicitly accept residual concerns outside protected risk classes. Acceptance must name the concern and consequence; silence, arbitrary round caps, and elapsed budget are not acceptance.
- The validation spike may use frozen historical changes and disposable worktrees. It does not change shipped review policy, reviewer roles, repository gates, or accepted contracts.
- Excludes duplicate-assurance removal, gate scheduling, progressive spec authoring, orchestration changes, learning or release bookkeeping, and backlog pruning; the parent intent owns those separate branches.

## Assumptions

- **Held, and measured.** After relevant bytes stabilize, later fresh broad reviews often add findings caused by prior repairs rather than materially independent defect coverage. Across all rounds, 24 of the baseline arms' sustained findings were attributed to an earlier repair against 4 in the candidate arms, with 4 repair-induced Blockers against 1. C1's baseline did not converge across four rounds, sustaining 3, then 3, then 7.
- **Held.** A focused reviewer given the sustained finding, fix, affected contract, and dependency context can verify closure as reliably as a fresh general review. On C1, C3, and C6 the focused re-review caught the defect its own arm's repair had introduced — proving one with a failing roster test and one by hashing rather than trusting the implementer's report.
- **Falsified for the combined candidate; causal attribution remains unproven.** Consequence-bound blocking plus focused re-review did not retain material coverage: the candidate missed three protected-class clusters. Because the experiment ran the mechanisms as a bundle with one first pass per arm, it cannot establish that consequence-bound blocking alone caused each miss rather than reviewer variance or another interaction. The blocking rule has nevertheless earned no independent safety evidence and should not be carried into a recut without an isolated test.
- **Falsified as a cost model.** Review cost is not concentrated only in broad resampling. On C3 and C4 it is concentrated in *repair*: those cases' repair calls dominate their arm totals (C4 17.4M baseline against 15.9M candidate tokens; C3 7.0M against 6.7M), and both arms pay it, which is why the median token saving is 29.73% rather than above 30%.
- **Newly measured, and outside this child's boundary.** A third of sustained findings — 22 of 67 — are unmet repository-convention obligations rather than logic defects, distributed almost evenly across the two arms, so no change to review scheduling reduces them. The [spike record](../research/work-loop-review-economics-spike.md#a-cost-driver-neither-policy-addresses) names the implementer brief as the existing seam.
- Existing completed changes contain enough review history to reconstruct findings. The 2026-09-09 corpus audit's finding that they lack usable model-token evidence was correct, and the owner-authorized method amendment resolved it by capturing both arms fresh rather than by waiting for six future changes.
- **Untested.** Maintainers will accept explicit, evidenced residual-risk decisions when protected risk classes remain blocking. The spike did not simulate owner authority, so this remains open.

## De-risking

- **Door:** Two-way. The proposed stopping contract can be tested on frozen inputs and disposable worktrees before any shipped policy changes.
- **Approach:** `validate-first`, overriding the two-way-door default because the probe changes an assurance boundary and must show savings and retained material coverage before implementation.
- **What would have to be true:** Review cost must be concentrated in broad resampling and the work it generates; focused re-review must retain material defect detection; revision validity must be decidable without adding comparable machinery; and maintainers must be able to accept non-protected residual risk explicitly.
- **Riskiest assumption:** Consequence-bound blocking plus focused re-review can materially reduce both review-stage wall-clock time and model tokens without missing findings whose consequences justify blocking delivery.
- **Kill condition (predeclared 2026-09-09):** Kill or reshape this candidate if a paired six-change spike misses any independently adjudicated sustained finding with a security, privacy, data-loss, migration, mixed-version, public-contract, destructive-operation, or human-approval consequence that the baseline found; misses more than one other sustained material blocker or high-severity finding; or fails to reduce both median review-stage wall-clock time and median total review, adjudication, and repair model tokens by at least 30% across the four non-high-risk cases.
- **Corpus condition:** Preselect two low-risk single-task changes, two ordinary full-mode single-owner changes, and two high-risk changes. Each case must have a frozen pre-review revision, the accepted contract, review and adjudication outputs, repair history, and usable timing and token evidence. If six eligible cases do not exist, stop and define the minimum measurement capture before running the comparison rather than substituting weaker cases after seeing results.
- **Spike activity:** Replay the candidate policy against each frozen input in an isolated disposable worktree, preserve the historical run as the baseline, independently adjudicate the union of baseline and candidate findings, and compare review-stage elapsed time, model tokens, material findings retained, repair-induced findings, and closure outcome. Report each risk stratum separately; do not blend a high-risk miss into an average saving.
- **Spike result: Killed on 2026-09-09.** The paired six-case spike ran to a verdict. Evidence: [work-loop review economics spike](../research/work-loop-review-economics-spike.md). **The clause that decided it** is the protected-class clause: the candidate missed three independently adjudicated sustained findings carrying a protected consequence that the baseline found — a High security finding on C4 (adversarial-reviewer's threat check replaced by routing plus "Do not emit a threat finding", while `security-reviewer` dispatches only on an enumerated boundary list that excludes ordinary prompt wording), a High public-contract finding on the high-risk case C6 (a published-behaviour change landing under the already-released 2.21.0), and a Medium public-contract finding on C3 (the versioned diagnostic vocabulary extended with no recorded authorization). **The cost condition also did not pass, but is not an independent basis for the verdict**: median total review, adjudication and repair tokens fell 29.73% across the four non-high-risk cases against a 30% bar, while the wall-clock half is not defensibly measurable on the host the run used. The earlier 2026-09-09 stop before replay is superseded by this verdict.

### Corpus audit and minimum capture

The repository contains review rounds, adjudication, repairs, revisions, and some elapsed-time evidence, but no completed change with recorded model-token usage. A bounded inventory found 26 spec directories with text matching the other five evidence categories and no token evidence. One apparent six-category match, `research-pack`, used “tokens” for literal routing strings rather than model usage. A repository-wide exact search for `input_tokens`, `output_tokens`, `cached_input_tokens`, `reasoning_output_tokens`, `total_tokens`, `tokens used`, `tokens consumed`, or `token usage` fields returned no matches. The eligible corpus is therefore 0 of the required 6 cases.

An execution probe on 2026-09-09 confirmed that the installed `codex exec --json` surface could provide a prospective runner in principle, but the process was refused before a model session because network access to `ab.chatgpt.com` is not on this environment's allowlist. One approved escalated retry returned the same refusal. No further retries were made, no credentials or protected configuration were inspected, and no case was partially recorded. The paired run is blocked until this environment admits that endpoint or provides another approved review runner with provider-reported token usage.

Before replay, capture six preselected cases without changing the kill condition:

1. Freeze two low-risk single-task changes, two ordinary full-mode single-owner changes, and two high-risk changes. Record each pre-review revision plus SHA-256 digests of the accepted contract and review subject.
2. Capture the current policy during six qualifying future work-loop cases as the historical baseline. Preserve each frozen pre-review input, then replay the candidate policy in a separate clean session. Pin the model, reviewer-role revision, tool configuration, and policy revision.
3. For every review, adjudication, repair, and re-review call, capture monotonic start and end times plus provider-reported input, cached-input, and output tokens. A text-length estimate is not token evidence.
4. Record every finding's stable identifier, severity, protected-risk class if any, traced consequence, disposition, originating arm, repair, and closure result. Independently adjudicate the union only after both arms finish.
5. Preserve per-case and per-risk-stratum results. Compute the predeclared medians only for the four non-high-risk cases, and report high-risk misses separately rather than averaging them away.

### Method amendment (2026-09-09, owner-authorized)

The owner authorized running six fresh paired baseline and candidate sessions now, against frozen completed changes, instead of waiting for six future work-loop cases to generate the baseline. This replaces item 2 of the capture list above. It changes nothing else: the corpus composition stays two low-risk single-task changes, two ordinary full-mode single-owner changes, and two high-risk changes; the guardrail stays intact; and the kill thresholds stay exactly as predeclared on 2026-09-09.

The amendment is admissible because the earlier stop was caused by missing token evidence in historical records, not by missing frozen inputs. Running both arms fresh produces provider-reported token and timing evidence for both arms under one pinned configuration, which is stronger than comparing a fresh candidate against an unmeasured historical baseline. It also removes the confound of comparing two different model and policy revisions.

Two conditions bound the amendment. A frozen case is admitted only when its pre-review revision, accepted contract, and bounded review subject are recoverable from repository history, and when its historical findings, adjudication, or repair evidence can be read for the missed-material-finding comparison. A case is never replaced after its results are seen.

**Telemetry mechanism.** Every dispatched agent writes its own JSONL transcript, and each assistant message in it carries the inference provider's own `usage` block — `input_tokens`, `cache_read_input_tokens`, `cache_creation_input_tokens`, and `output_tokens` — for the API request that produced it. A call's cost is the sum of those blocks across the call's requests. Tokens are exact provider-reported integers, never text-length estimates. Elapsed time per call comes from the transcript's first and last record timestamps at millisecond resolution, cross-checked on the first call against the host's independently reported duration to within 50 ms. The host's two summary fields were tested and rejected: a synchronous call's `toolUseResult.totalTokens` reports only the final request, and an asynchronous call — this host's dispatch mode — omits `usage` entirely and reports one aggregate with no input, cached-input, or output split. The [spike record](../research/work-loop-review-economics-spike.md#telemetry-mechanism-and-precision) holds the verification detail.

```yaml
validation_hook:
  assumption: Consequence-bound blocking plus focused re-review can cut review-stage wall-clock time and model tokens without losing material defect coverage.
  kill_condition: Kill or reshape on any missed protected-class sustained finding, more than one other missed sustained material blocker or high-severity finding, or less than 30% median savings in either wall-clock time or model tokens across the four non-high-risk cases.
  activity: Enable an approved token-reporting review runner, capture the missing timing and token evidence for six preselected paired cases, run the replay, have an independent reviewer adjudicate the union of findings, and obtain the maintainer's judgment on whether the observed residual risks are acceptable.
  status: run 2026-09-09; killed on the protected-class clause. The token half missed its bar by 0.27 percentage points and wall clock was not defensibly measurable, so cost is not an independent basis for the verdict. Evidence in docs/product/research/work-loop-review-economics-spike.md. The maintainer-judgment half of the activity was deliberately not simulated and remains open.
```

## Projection

- The spike was killed, so no delivery brief is projected from this intent as shaped. The protected-class clause decided the verdict. The token half missed its bar by 0.27 percentage points and wall clock was not defensibly measurable, so the cost condition is recorded but not treated as an independent basis. The [spike record](../research/work-loop-review-economics-spike.md#survivekill-calculation) holds the calculation.
- **Owner disposition (2026-09-09):** Recut rather than drop. The replacement sibling is [work-loop focused re-review](work-loop-focused-re-review.md), which preserves initial broad review and existing blocking semantics while testing post-repair focus in isolation. Consequence-bound blocking and revision-bound reuse do not carry into that child.
- The result returns to the parent before this branch is replaced or recut. Two separable parts of the bet survived measurement and are the material a recut would start from: focused re-review of sustained findings, their fixes and affected contract bytes retained closure verification and caught three repair-induced defects; and the baseline's later broad rounds did mostly resample their own repairs, 24 repair-induced sustained findings against the candidate's 4. What failed is the combined candidate and its cost model, because it missed three protected-class clusters and two of four non-high-risk cases are repair-dominated rather than review-dominated. The one-run-per-arm design does not isolate consequence-bound blocking as the cause of every miss.
- Any recut needs its own predeclared kill condition. Do not compensate by weakening the predeclared guardrail: the three missed findings were security, public-contract, and public-contract consequences, exactly the classes the guardrail names as remaining blocking.
- One measured finding belongs to the parent rather than to any recut of this child: a third of sustained findings are unmet repository conventions, split evenly across both arms, so review scheduling cannot reach them. The parent's repair-correctness branch (`Impact 2`) owns post-adjudication obligation delivery, while progressive authoring (`Impact 7`) and the existing `agent-authoring-input-quality` brief own upstream spec and plan quality.
