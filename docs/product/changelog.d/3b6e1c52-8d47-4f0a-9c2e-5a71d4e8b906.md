## Highlights
- A `run-concurrent` request can now name several tasks, up to the `max_concurrent_attempts` policy value: the first tasks in the order the sequential loop would issue them whose declared reads and writes do not meet. Each attempt runs in its own directory with its own launcher, and the verb settles each one as it finishes.
- Results integrate one at a time, in the sequential order, under one lock in the run store. A result that finishes early is held, recorded by its tree and the digest of its access line, until the tasks before it have landed; a later `next` or `report` lands any held result that has come up. A result is checked against what changed since its source commit, then merged by a pinned three-way merge that no attribute or configuration can steer. A merge conflict, a merge setting, an attested read or write that meets a change, or a changed symbolic link target gives a conflict record, leaves the product ref untouched, and projects the task again.
- A killed run converges on the next verb: a landed result is recognised by its commit and recorded without a second commit.
