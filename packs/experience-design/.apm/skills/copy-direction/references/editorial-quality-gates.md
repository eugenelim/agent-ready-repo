# Editorial quality gates — product-copy mode

> Note: this reference is intentionally duplicated from `tone-of-voice`'s
> `references/editorial-quality-gates.md`. Skill autonomy beats DRY at this
> scale — each skill stands alone.

Apply these gates when the upstream content brief declares `communication_mode: product-copy`. They are judgment tools, not checklists. A "flag" is a prompt to examine whether the word or pattern is earning its place.

---

## Anti-AI-smell scan

The following words are warning signals. Each one earns the question:
**"Is this word carrying a specific meaning, or compensating for a lack of specificity?"**

If it is compensating: replace it with the specific claim it is hiding.

**Warning signal words:** unlock, empower, seamless, robust, comprehensive, powerful,
innovative, transformative, streamlined, leverage, next-generation, best-in-class,
end-to-end, at scale, revolutionize, cutting-edge, game-changing, world-class.

**Structural warning signals — flag and examine:**
- Generic three-part lists (X, Y, and Z — where X/Y/Z are interchangeable abstractions)
- Repeated triads (three bullets, three features, three benefits — all similar shape)
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions
- "Whether you're X or Y" openings
- Long paragraphs reducible to one sentence
- Multiple paragraphs saying essentially the same thing
- Copy that could describe 10,000 other SaaS products unchanged
- Claims without evidence
- Features described without naming the reader's outcome

---

## Deletion pass — assume 30% too long

Work through these questions. Remove anything that does not increase understanding,
desire, credibility, differentiation, or action.

1. Can the opening be stronger?
2. Can the first paragraph be cut in half?
3. Is the strongest idea appearing early enough?
4. Are we saying the same thing twice?
5. Are we explaining a feature instead of naming the outcome?
6. Can this paragraph become one sentence?
7. Can this section disappear entirely?
8. Does this heading earn its existence?
9. Does this bullet list improve scanning, or would a sentence serve better?
10. Is the product's actual point of view visible?

---

## Human copy tests

Run all four. Note which passed and which raised a concern.

**5-second test** — after 5 seconds, can a reader answer all six:
1. What is this?
2. Who is it for?
3. Why should I care?
4. Why is it different?
5. Why should I believe it?
6. What should I do next?

**Specificity test** — could this paragraph be pasted onto another company's website
with only the product name changed? If yes, rewrite until it cannot.

**Point-of-view test** — does the copy have a clear opinion about:
- the problem (not just that it exists)
- the status quo (why current approaches fail)
- what should be done differently?
If not, the copy is informative but not persuasive.

**Distinctiveness test** — does this sound like a specific human who understands
the customer and has something specific to say? Or does it sound like an AI
explaining a category? Prefer the former.
