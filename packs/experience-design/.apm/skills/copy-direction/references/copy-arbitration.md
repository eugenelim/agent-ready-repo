# Copy arbitration for per-surface direction

Named copy goals will pull a single copy choice in opposite directions for a specific surface. Arbitration is how you decide *before* the conflict reaches a copy choice, so the team resolves it once rather than re-litigating it on every draft.

## The rule: rank, then a tie has an answer

Two goals of equal weight cannot break a tie. **Rank the goals up front** and name a **dominant goal**: the one that wins when goals genuinely conflict on a real copy choice for this surface. Ranking is the whole mechanism.

## Common conflict types

**Urgency vs. warmth.** A time-sensitive CTA ("Start today — pricing changes next week") conflicts with a warm, unhurried register. On a bottom-of-funnel acquisition surface, urgency typically wins; on a welcome surface read at varied paces, warmth typically wins.

**Brevity vs. completeness.** A tweet-test goal conflicts with a goal of handling every reader objection. Above-fold copy for a cold audience typically favours brevity; a pricing comparison table for an evaluating buyer typically favours completeness.

**Authority vs. approachability.** A "guide-not-guru" goal wants a peer register; an "earned authority" goal wants expertise that precedes the claim. These conflict when the audience is mixed across seniority.

**Specificity vs. universality.** A specific claim ("Designed for teams of five to fifty") excludes readers outside that frame; a universal claim converts no one precisely. Name which wins for this surface and audience.

## How to rank

1. **Order by what the copy cannot survive losing.** Ask, goal by goal: "If we nailed everything except this one, would the copy still do its job on this surface?" The goal that breaks the copy when missing sits at the top.
2. **Force a strict order.** No ties at the top. If two goals feel equal, find the specific copy choice where they would conflict and decide which you would sacrifice.
3. **Sanity-check against the felt word.** The dominant goal should match the user's original gut answer from Stage 1 of the interrogation.

## Record the trade-off, not just the winner

When you resolve a conflict, write down all three parts:

- **The tension** — which two goals pull apart, and on what kind of copy choice (CTA phrasing, headline length, level of proof before the claim).
- **The call** — which goal wins: "When brevity and completeness conflict on above-fold copy, brevity wins."
- **The why** — one sentence tying it to the ranking.

A recorded trade-off turns a recurring argument into a settled default. The build can still escalate a specific case — but the burden is on the exception to justify itself.
