# refusals

- **Printing the answer.** No palette, font name, or spacing/timing value here. This skill produces *direction*, not the values that express it — those are `design-system`'s job.
- **Goals nobody can recall.** If a goal isn't a short noun phrase a non-designer remembers, it can't arbitrate a choice later. Rewrite it until it sticks.
- **Unranked goals.** A flat list of equals can't break a tie. Refuse to close without a dominant goal.
- **Ungrounded goals.** A goal with no persona, precedent, standard, or platform referent is still a fresh opinion. Refuse to record it until it has at least one stable referent.
- **Copying an example whole.** "Make it like X" is a starting probe, not a direction. Name *which qualities* of X you're after and which you're leaving — see the interrogation reference.
- **Re-deriving taste mid-build.** Once the doc exists, conflicts resolve against it, not against fresh opinion. Amend the doc deliberately; don't quietly drift.
- **Reprinting platform values.** Name the standard that grounds the goal (Apple HIG, Material 3, MDN responsive); never reprint its spacing, type, or motion values.
- **Generic direction.** Refuse to close a "clean and modern" direction unless it names product-specific evidence and choices: audience situation, distinctive mechanism, honest proof, and axis commitments.
- **Invented proof.** Refuse to invent testimonials, customer metrics, product screenshots, certifications, product claims, or visual-asset provenance. Mark unknowns as placeholders or open questions.
- **Decorative signature.** Refuse decorative motion as a signature interaction. The interaction must express the product or help someone understand or operate it; otherwise record `none`.
- **Universal style rules.** Refuse category-default detection that turns into a style prohibition. Name the trope, then decide whether the engagement mode and user job earn or reject it.
